# Continuum demo video — creative brief

## Objective

Produce a roughly four-minute, technically credible presentation that makes a
judge understand and remember one idea:

> Agents can fail. Their obligations must not.

The video is a live product demonstration, not a feature tour. It must prove
autonomous operational value, disciplined Google Cloud architecture, and a
verifiable outcome within one coherent procurement-succession incident.

## Audience and desired reaction

The primary audience is an All Things Agentic judge evaluating many agent demos
in succession. By the end, the judge should be able to say:

1. Continuum detects an important event that did not happen.
2. It safely transfers an obligation and minimum authorized context to an
   eligible successor selected with Gemini.
3. The former agent is fenced, duplicate delivery does not duplicate the
   external effect, and an independent verifier proves the outcome.
4. This is a portable continuity protocol demonstrated through a production-
   minded Google Cloud reference binding—not merely a chatbot or workflow UI.

## Narrative thesis

A procurement agent has made a EUR 250,000 onboarding commitment. The expected
compliance evidence never arrives, but there is no ordinary error to catch.
Continuum observes the absence, investigates it, governs succession, transfers
only authorized context, completes the obligation exactly once within the
demonstrated boundary, and independently verifies the evidence chain.

## Emotional arc

1. **Concern** — a valuable obligation is silently at risk.
2. **Surprise** — Continuum detects what did not happen.
3. **Intelligence** — Gemini reasons over a deterministic eligible set and
   cites live candidate evidence.
4. **Trust** — the old identity and its memory access are rejected; poisoned,
   secret, inferred, and revoked context do not cross the boundary.
5. **Relief** — one externally observed effect survives redelivery and agent
   replacement.
6. **Proof** — a separate read-only verifier, not the executor, issues the final
   attestation.
7. **Ambition with humility** — Continuum is proposed as a future standard that
   should itself eventually yield to a demonstrably stronger one.

## Tone

- Technically precise, calm, and confident.
- Human stakes before architecture.
- Dry wit no more than three times; never during the final security claim.
- No inflated guarantees. Scope every statement to the demonstrated gateway,
  identity, provider, and failure model.
- Narration should sound spoken, not like documentation being read aloud.

Candidate dry-wit lines:

- “The browser gets one click—and no vote.”
- “Pub/Sub delivers twice. The vendor is still created once. Retries are useful;
  duplicate procurement is less charming.”
- “The old agent can try. Continuum can say no.”

## Signature moments

Primary product payoff:

> The agent failed. The EUR 250,000 promise did not.

Personal closing thought:

> I built Continuum because useful agents need more than intelligence;
> institutions need continuity. I hope this becomes a standard—until a stronger
> standard earns the right to succeed it. Even standards should have succession
> plans.

The final script may shorten this thought to fit the live timing, but must
preserve its meaning and understated wordplay.

## Structural decision

Do not lead with the architecture diagram. First establish the incident and
start a fresh canonical run. Reveal architecture near the end, after the viewer
has seen the behavior it explains. Google Cloud services then function as
evidence rather than a technology checklist.

## Production constraints

- Target 3:40–3:50, leaving a small margin below four minutes.
- Prefer one continuous screen-recorded presentation or transparently marked
  continuous segments only if the official rules allow editing.
- Start exactly one fresh canonical run and expose its run and trace IDs.
- The browser may initiate and observe; server-side state drives the lifecycle.
- Pre-open authenticated evidence views, but do not substitute old artifacts
  for the fresh run.
- If a dependency stalls, show the pending or fail-closed state honestly.
- Every visible identifier must remain legible at normal video resolution.

## Narration direction

Use an English voice that is warm, restrained, and authoritative rather than a
cinematic trailer voice. Medium pace, short pauses after the opening thesis and
signature payoff, and slightly faster delivery only during architecture proof.
Technical identifiers should be articulated cleanly without reading full hashes.
